# TS-1 protocol: a 5-working-day timeliness screen (descriptive; not a legal finding)

**Status: fixed before any gap is computed.** This protocol is committed and tagged `ts-protocol-1` before the screen runs. Any later change goes in `reports/TS_AMENDMENTS.md`, with its date and reason, and every result it affects is labelled **POST HOC**.

**Every output is a silver reference (AI-generated, not human-verified).** The decision dates come from AI-made silver labels or from the frozen agent's accepted claims.

**Hard rules:**
- No model runs.
- Nothing under `freeze-v1` or `amendment-1` changes.
- Read-only use of the run-1/run-2 outputs, the frozen gate decisions, the silver labels, the cached PDFs and parsed text, and the NHTSA flat file.
- Aggregates only: no company names and no recall numbers in any output table. Manufacturers are anonymised as M1..Mk.

The inputs are described in `reports/TS_INVENTORY.md` (commit c3d1f97 and the follow-up rename commit).

## A. Legal basis and what the screen can and cannot say

Quoted verbatim from the eCFR, Title 49 Part 573, as of 2026-10-23 (fetched with the eCFR versioner API on 2026-10-28):

> **§ 573.6(b)** "Each report shall be submitted not more than 5 working days after a defect in a vehicle or item of equipment has been determined to be safety related, or a noncompliance with a motor vehicle safety standard has been determined to exist. At a minimum, information required by paragraphs (c)(1), (2), and (5) of this section shall be submitted in the initial report. The remainder of the information required by paragraph (c) of this section that is not available within the five-day period shall be submitted within 5 working days after the manufacturer has confirmed the accuracy of the information. In addition, each manufacturer shall amend information required by paragraphs (c)(2), (3), and (8)(i) or (ii) within 5 working days after it has new information that updates or corrects information that was previously reported. Each manufacturer submitting new information relative to a previously submitted report shall refer to the recall campaign number when a number has been assigned by the NHTSA."

> **§ 573.6(c)(6)** "In the case of a defect, a chronology of all principal events that were the basis for the determination that the defect related to motor vehicle safety, including a summary of all warranty claims, field or service reports, and other information, with their dates of receipt."

> **§ 573.6(c)(7)** "In the case of a noncompliance, the test results and other information that the manufacturer considered in determining the existence of the noncompliance. The manufacturer shall identify the date of each test and observation that indicated that a noncompliance might or did exist."

> **§ 573.6(c)(8)(iii)** (second sentence) "If such a petition is filed and subsequently denied, the manufacturer shall provide the information required by paragraph (c)(8)(ii) of this section within five Federal government business days from the date the petition denial is published in the Federal Register."

> **§ 573.7(d)** (opening) "The reports required by this section shall be submitted in accordance with the following schedule, except that if the due date specified below falls on a Saturday, Sunday or Federal holiday, the report shall be submitted on the next day that is a business day for the Federal government:"

Stated plainly:

1. **A screening signal, not a legal finding.** The gap computed here is a screening signal. It is not a finding that any filing was out of time, and no result may be worded as one.
2. **A lower bound at best.** The legally relevant trigger can be when the manufacturer *should have* determined that a safety-related defect exists. That can come before every date in the chronology. The screen measures only the **self-reported** interval from the stated decision to the submission, which is at best a lower bound on the legally relevant interval. It also depends on how each filer words and dates its chronology.
3. **The working-day definition is an assumption.** Part 573 does not define "working days". We **assume** Monday–Friday excluding US federal holidays, by analogy with § 573.7(d) and § 573.6(c)(8)(iii), which both tie business days to the federal calendar.

## B. Holidays

**Table:** `data/holidays_us_federal.csv`, generated by code (`scripts/ts_holidays.py` using `holiday_rows` in `scripts/ts_lib.py`). It has 492 rows for 1980–2028, SHA-256 `cb00b3ac48d335e16abbfea456985153a396b4f353dd2bfcf8ad920b47d0df81`.

**Rules (5 U.S.C. 6103):**
- **Fixed-date holidays:** New Year's Day (Jan 1), Independence Day (Jul 4), Veterans Day (Nov 11), Christmas Day (Dec 25).
- **Monday holidays:** Washington's Birthday (3rd Monday of February), Memorial Day (last Monday of May), Labor Day (1st Monday of September), Columbus Day (2nd Monday of October).
- **Thanksgiving Day:** 4th Thursday of November.
- **Birthday of Martin Luther King, Jr.:** 3rd Monday of January, from 1986.
- **Juneteenth National Independence Day:** Jun 19, **only from 2021**.
- **Observed-date rule (§ 6103(b)):** a Saturday holiday is observed on the Friday before, and a Sunday holiday on the Monday after. Observed dates can fall in the previous year; for example New Year's Day 2022 was observed on 2021-12-31.

**Not included, and listed as a limitation:** one-off executive-order closures (for example extra days off around Christmas, or days of mourning), and Inauguration Day (§ 6103(c), which applies to the DC area only).

**Unit tests** check 5 known observed dates: 2021-12-31 (New Year's Day 2022), 2021-06-18 (Juneteenth 2021), 2020-07-03 (Independence Day 2020), 2022-12-26 (Christmas Day 2022), 2023-11-10 (Veterans Day 2023).

## C. Filing date

- **Primary:** the PDF "Submission Date", read with the Step 0 regex `Submission\s*Date\s*:\s*([A-Za-z]{3,9})\.?\s+(\d{1,2}),\s*(\d{4})`. A filing with no parseable Submission Date is **NO_FILING_DATE**; Step 0 found none.
- **Secondary:** flat-file RCDATE ("Part 573 Defect/Noncompliance Report Received Date"), used only in the sensitivity runs and in (f).
- **Source:** the source used is recorded for every filing.
- **Caveat on amended versions** (Step 0 item 6): 213 of the 397 processed PDFs are a later, amended version. Their Submission Date may be the amendment's date, not the date of the initial report. That would lengthen the gap.
- **No correction:** the primary analysis does not correct for this. The RCDATE and version sensitivities in H(d), and the RCDATE comparison in H(f), show how much it matters.

## D. Dates as intervals

**What gets a date.** Every event date is re-parsed from its raw `date_text` with one parser (`resolve` in `scripts/ts_lib.py`), for silver and agent alike. The stored silver span is not used because it is missing for Pass-C items (Step 0 item 5). An event with an empty `date_text`, or a text the parser does not read, has **no date** and cannot be an anchor.

**Which silver events are used.** Only non-contested silver events are used. Events whose `date_text` is an inherited chronology-entry header date are used, since that is the date written for the entry.

**Precision and interval [lo, hi]:**
| precision | interval |
|---|---|
| day | that day; also ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` and dates such as "20. Jan 2023" or "16-Sep-2026" |
| iso_week | Monday–Friday of the ISO week: "CW51/2020" (also KW/WK/"week N"), and "week of <date>" (the week containing that date) |
| month | the whole month |
| month part | first third (days 1–10, "early …", "beginning of …", "start of …"); middle third (11–20, "mid …", "middle of …"); final third (21 to month end, "end of …" and the synonymous adjective) |
| quarter | the quarter ("Q1 2024", "1Q 2024", "first quarter of 2024") |
| year part | an addition to the list: the same three words applied to a year give Jan–Apr, May–Aug or Sep–Dec |
| year | the whole year |
| range | an addition to the list: two dates joined by through/thru/until/till/to/and/& or a dash give [lo of the first, hi of the second]. A start without a year takes the end's year, or the year before if it would otherwise fall after the end |

**Reading rules:**
- **Prefixes are stripped:** "on or about", "on or around", "in or about", "approximately", "around", "about", "circa", "between", "from", "since", "during", "as of", "on", "in", "by", "the".
- **Year-less dates** ("May 16", "March", "9/5") take their year by the frozen labelling rule (`tt.labels.dates.parse_span`):
  - the single year of the chronology's fully dated events (`tt.labels.events.chronology_years`, read-only);
  - else, when there is no such year at all, the filing year, provided the result is not after the filing date;
  - otherwise the event has **no date**.
- **Ambiguous numeric dates.** When a numeric date such as 03/04/2023 reads validly as both mm/dd and dd/mm, keep each reading whose lo is ≤ the filing date (all readings if none qualifies). If two distinct readings remain, use their union interval and mark it **AMBIGUOUS**. A numeric date with a first number above 12 reads only as dd/mm; one with a second number above 12 only as mm/dd.
- **Cap:** if hi is after the filing date and lo is not, hi is capped at the filing date.
- **Data with no ISO-only events:** Step 0 found no ISO-only event. The day-precision rule for ISO-only events is kept but never applies.

## E. Decision anchor (primary)

**Rule:** the latest DECISION-typed event that meets all three conditions:
- it has a date;
- its lo is ≤ the filing date;
- its quote does **not** cite a different recall number. The regex is `\d{2}[VECT]-?\d{3}`, case-sensitive, as specified; a match counts as different if it is not equal to the current campaign once the hyphen is removed. Quotes exist for every event (Step 0), so the rule always runs.

"Latest" means the largest hi, then the largest lo; among intervals tied on both, any one gives the same gap.

**Two versions of the anchor:**
- **Reference:** the silver labels.
- **Agent:** run-1 event claims ACCEPTED by the frozen gate (score ≥ 3: no verifier fired and run 2 agrees), extracted read-only by `scripts/ts_gate.py`.

If no event qualifies, the status is **NO_DECISION_DATE**. A date is never imputed.

## F. Gap and flag

- **WD(a → b)** = the number of working days d with a < d ≤ b, where a working day is Monday–Friday and not an observed holiday from B. It is 0 if b ≤ a.
- **gap_min** = WD(hi → filing). **gap_max** = WD(lo → filing). **gap_mid** = (gap_min + gap_max) / 2.
- **Flag:**
  - **FLAGGED** if gap_min > 5;
  - **NOT_FLAGGED** if gap_max ≤ 5;
  - otherwise **INDETERMINATE**.
- **Unit tests** (they must pass before any result is written):
  - same day = 0;
  - Monday → next Monday = 5;
  - Friday → next Monday = 1;
  - a Saturday decision (Saturday → Monday = 1);
  - a span over an observed holiday (Thursday 2020-07-02 → Monday 2020-07-06 = 1, because Friday 2020-07-03 is the observed Independence Day);
  - a month interval giving INDETERMINATE.

## G. Population

**The primary analysis covers defect filings.** The type is decided in this order (Step 0 item 4):
1. the report text header ("Description of Defect :" / "Description of Noncompliance :", pre-2025 form);
2. else the NHTSA document title in the version listing (never needed here);
3. else the flat-file FMVSS proxy: empty → defect, non-empty → noncompliance.

The proxy types every 2025+-form filing (186 of 397, including all of Test-B). The type source is recorded for each filing, and a pre-declared sensitivity drops the proxy-typed filings (H(d)).

**Noncompliance filings** are reported separately and only descriptively, with table (a) only. § 573.6(c)(6) asks for a chronology for defects only, and noncompliance reports carry test dates instead.

## H. Outputs (per split and pooled over all three; the splits are disjoint sets of campaigns)

**General definitions:**
- **N** = defect filings with a filing date.
- **Status categories:** FLAGGED, NOT_FLAGGED, INDETERMINATE, NO_DECISION_DATE.
- **Clusters:** a filer cluster is the filer name from the selection file, lower-cased with whitespace collapsed. The pooled analysis uses the same key across splits.
- **Bootstrap:** the frozen `tt.eval.riskcov.cluster_bootstrap` (2,000 resamples, seed 0, percentile 95%), imported read-only.
- **Quantiles:** linear interpolation between order statistics.

**(a) Reference (silver):**
- n with a decision date.
- Median, IQR and p90 of gap_mid, and the same for gap_min and gap_max, over filings with a decision date.
- The four status shares over N, each with a Wilson 95% CI and a filer-cluster bootstrap CI.
- The number of AMBIGUOUS anchors and the anchors' precision mix.

**(b) Agent:**
- **Coverage:** the share of N with an agent decision date.
- **Complete-case shares:** the FLAGGED share (and the INDETERMINATE share) among filings with an agent decision date.
- **Worst-case (Manski) bounds** on the FLAGGED share over all N:
  - **lower bound:** agent FLAGGED / N, with every unknown counted as not flagged;
  - **upper bound:** (agent FLAGGED + unknown) / N, with every unknown counted as flagged.
  - Unknown = no accepted DECISION claim (abstention), NO_DECISION_DATE, or INDETERMINATE. INDETERMINATE is counted as unknown because it can be either; the counts of each are shown, so the bounds can be recomputed without it.
- Cluster bootstrap CIs for coverage and for both bounds.

**(c) Agreement (filings where both have a decision date):**
- **Exact date agreement:** identical [lo, hi], and separately "both day precision and the same day".
- **|Δ| = |gap_mid(agent) − gap_mid(reference)|** in working days: median, p90, share = 0, share ≤ 5.
- **Flag table:** the 3×3 table with raw counts.
- **Precision and recall** of agent FLAGGED against reference FLAGGED.
- **Agreement statistics:** Cohen's κ and PABAK (3 categories, (3·p_o − 1)/2).
- **CIs:** cluster bootstrap CIs for exact agreement, precision, recall, κ and PABAK.
- **Instability note:** if fewer than 10 such filings are reference FLAGGED, the report says the estimates are unstable.

**(d) Pre-declared sensitivity runs.** For each variant, report the share of N whose status changes against the primary, for the reference and for the agent, plus the variant's status shares.
1. **Calendar days** instead of working days (same threshold, 5).
2. **Holidays not excluded:** Monday–Friday only.
3. **Earliest DECISION** instead of the latest.
4. **Latest event of ANY type** with lo ≤ the filing date.
5. **Lexicon:** the latest event of any type whose quote contains (case-insensitive) one of: decided to conduct, decided to recall, determined that a defect, determination, Recall Determination Committee, Product Safety Committee, Field Review Committee, Safety Committee. Quotes exist, so this runs.
6. **RCDATE** instead of the Submission Date as the filing date. Anchors, caps and year-less years are recomputed against it.
7. **Original vs amended version.** Step 0 showed it is knowable but that the original's own Submission Date is not available. The variant therefore reports the primary status shares separately for filings whose processed PDF is the original (only version) and for those processed from an amended version. Status changes do not apply to it.
8. **An addition, declared here before any result:** drop the filings typed by the FMVSS proxy, keeping only the type decided by the report text or the NHTSA title.

Any variant that cannot run is listed as "not run" with the reason.

**(e) Consistency audit (no ground truth).** For silver and agent-accepted events separately, the share of N with at least one of:
- **order inconsistency in the chain** FILER_AWARENESS ≤ INVESTIGATION_START ≤ DECISION ≤ filing date. An event X of an earlier type has X.lo > Y.hi for an event Y of a later type, or a DECISION has lo > the filing date;
- **any dated event after the filing date** (lo > filing);
- **a DECISION more than 10 years before the filing** (hi < the filing date minus 10 calendar years).

Only events with a date count. The audit is descriptive.

**(f) Descriptive:**
- **How informative the screen is:** the share of N whose latest dated event (lo ≤ filing, by hi then lo) is DECISION-typed (counted if any event tied for latest is a DECISION), for reference and agent.
- **Submission Date vs RCDATE:** the share exactly equal, and the distribution of RCDATE − Submission Date in calendar days (min, p10, median, p90, max, and the counts below, at and above 0). This is shown for all filings, by split, and separately for original and amended processed versions.
- **Strata:** reference status shares stratified by INFLUENCED_BY and RCLTYPECD (both exist), with n and Wilson CIs.
- **Manufacturers:** per-manufacturer rows only for manufacturers with ≥ 10 pooled defect filings, anonymised M1..Mk. The order is by count, ties broken by a hash of the name.

## I. Reporting
- Aggregates only; no company names or recall numbers in any table.
- The results are reported whatever they show.
- The words used are "FLAGGED", "NOT_FLAGGED", "INDETERMINATE" and "screening signal". No result is described as a legal conclusion about any filing or filer.
- Per-filing intermediate files (`runs/ts/`, gitignored) stay out of the repository, because they carry recall numbers.

## J. Prior art (for the report)
- **Closest regulatory analogue:** NHTSA's case-by-case timeliness and audit queries. One is TQ14-001, whose consent order (May 2014) is on nhtsa.gov; another is AQ23-002, whose information-request documents are on static.nhtsa.gov. We found no aggregate across manufacturers published by NHTSA.
- **Closest method analogue:** Zhyrko, Han & Verberne (2026), "Time as Structure: Temporal Dependency Graphs for Verifiable Deadline Computation over Legal Documents", arXiv 2608.15270 (a preprint). It uses an extract-then-compute design: a model extracts dated facts, and a calendar engine does the arithmetic.
- **Closest data analogue:** Astvansh, Ball & Josefy (2022), MSOM 24(3):1457–1473, which hand-codes recall timing (awareness to recall) for 2,120 recalls by 27 automakers, 2009–2018.
- **Search scope:** four general web searches on 2026-10-28, read at snippet or summary level only:
  - the preprint by author name and arXiv id (twice);
  - "NHTSA timeliness query TQ14-001 Part 573 five working days";
  - "analysis of time between recall decision and Part 573 report submission across manufacturers NHTSA chronology".
  No bibliographic database (Scopus, Web of Science, SSRN, Google Scholar) was searched systematically, and no paper was read in full.
- **The only claim allowed:** "within this search scope, we found no prior work that computes this gap across multi-manufacturer Part 573 filings". The method is not claimed to be new: interval dates, working-day counting and extract-then-compute are all existing ideas.
